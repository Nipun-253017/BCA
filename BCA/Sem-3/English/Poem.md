Stanza 1 ("No, not a single letter is seen...")
The poet points out a harsh reality: while there are countless history books written about many different groups of people around the world, there is absolutely no written record of his own community. They have been completely ignored by historians.
Stanza 2 ("Scrutinize each one of them...")
He emphasizes this erasure by challenging the reader to look closely at all the world's history books. He states that no matter how deep you look into the past, you will not find even a single word acknowledging the existence or contributions of his people.
Stanza 3 ("There was no one on this earth...")
He expresses deep sorrow ("What a pity!") over the fact that in ancient times, nobody with the privilege and power to write history cared enough to record the lives of his ancestors. They were deliberately left out of the historical record by the dominant society.
Stanza 4 ("Think of it...")
Just to recap: The poet expresses sadness over this erasure, but decides to take action. Since nobody else wrote his people's history, he decides to sing and record it himself using his own voice and oral traditions.
Stanza 5 ("The story of...")
Just to recap: He announces the story he will tell: how his people, the original ancient inhabitants of Kerala, were unfairly stripped of their dignity and treated like outcasts or "demons" by powerful, invading groups.
Stanza 6 ("No shame have I...")
The poet boldly states he is not ashamed of his identity, even though society calls his people a "cursed offspring." By offering to talk about the "faults of my caste," he is using heavy sarcasm to mock the upper castes for unfairly blaming his people for their own oppression.
Stanza 7 ("How is it possible...")
He expresses frustration and disbelief at the sheer scale and length of this injustice. He wonders how it is even logically possible that society has universally agreed to blame and punish his people forever, right up to the "end of earth and sky."
Stanza 8 ("How can God...")
The poem concludes with a powerful, desperate question directed at the creator. The poet challenges God, asking how a supreme being who created the entire universe could just sit back and allow such cruel injustice and discrimination to continue happening on earth.
