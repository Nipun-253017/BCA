## Offer & Acceptance
### Offer (Proposal)
- An offer is the expression of one person willingness to do or abstain doing something, made with intention of obtaining the agreement of another person.
- The person making the offer is called **offer** and the person to whom the offer is made is called the **offeree**.

#### Types of Offer
1. **Express Offer:**
	- Offer made clearly through ==spoken or written== words are known as Express offer.
	- **Example:**
		- A sends a message that he is willing to sell his laptop for 40,000.
2. **Implied Offer:**
	- It is made ==through action==, not through spoken or written words.
	- **Example:**
		- A bus stops and open its door to imply an offer to carry the passengers.
3. **Specific Offer:**
	- It is ==addressed to a particular person or group==, only that person or group may accept the offer.
	- **Example:**
		- A offers to sell all of his property only to B.
4. **General Offer:**
	- Offer made to the public, anyone who fulfills the obligation may accept the offer .
	- 10,000 reward offered for finding  a lost time.
5. **Cross Offer:**
	- Two person simultaneously make identical offer to each  other without knowing about others offer.
	- **Example:**
		- A writes to B offering to sell a car of 5 lakh. At the same time B writes to A offering to buy the same car for 5 lakh without the knowledge of A offer.
6. **Counter Offer:**
	- When the person to whom offers is made come with a modification or change in the offer, it becomes a new offer.
	- **Example:**
		- A offers to sell a phone for 20K B replies that he will buy it for 18k.
7. **Standing Offer:**
	- An offer which made for longer period of time is known as **Standing offer**.
	- **Example:**
		- A offers to supply rice to B at 75 per kg over 12 months.

#### Essential Condition for a Valid offer
1. **Capable of creating legal Relationship**
	- There should be an intension to have legal relation, social gathering or carnival  invitation typically don't create legal relationships. Bementic and social  offer doesn't come under legal relationship.
2. **Terms must be Definite Certain & Clear**
	- If the terms of the offer are not clear the offer becomes invalid.
3. **Offer must be communicated  to the offerese**
	- An offer is  effective only when it's communicated to the person it's being made to.
4. **Offer may be Conditional**
	- A conditional offer can be accepted only subject to that condition. It lapses when condition is not accepted.

#### Lapse / Revocation of Offer
1. **By Notice of Revocation:**
     - As long as the offer has'nt been accepted, the offerer is free to revoke his offer anytime.
2. **After Stipulated/ Reasonable Time**
	- An offer lapes if acceptance is not communicated within the prescribed or within a reasonable time.
3. **An offer lapes by Rejection:**
	- When an offer is rejected by the offere it expires and can't be accepted later.
	- The rejection maybe expressed or implied.
4. **By Failure to Accept Condition:**
     - If the condition attached with the offer is not accepted, then the offer becomes lapsed.

### Acception
- Acception means giving a consent to a offer made by another party.
- The **identity of the accepted** depends upon wether the offer is specific or general in nature.
- **In specific offer:**
     - Acception can be done only by that identify person.
     - **Example:** A offer to sell his car to B for ₹5 lakh. 
     - Only B can accept the offer, no one else can do that Acception.
- **In general offer:**
     - Any member of the public who fullfil the contract may accept it.
     - **Example:** A announces reward for finding his lost document.
     - anyone who finds and returns the document can claim the reward.
- **Acception can be created 3 forms:**  
     - **Express Acception:** 
        - that is Acception communicated through spoken or written words.
	- **Implied Acception:**
	    - demonstrated through action without words.
	- **Conditional Acception:**
	     - subject to new condition, it is not valid acceptance. Such acceptance are treated as counter offer.
	     - **Example:** A offers goods for ₹10,000 to B. But B says that He will accept it only if he can pay in installments. Where it is a counter offer, not acceptance.
	
#### Essential Elements of acceptance
1. **Acceptance must be absolute:**
	- Acceptance  must be given in a way exactly as the offer, any modification, even minor converts its acceptance into counter offer.
2. **Must Be Communicated:**
	- The offers must naturally receive the acceptance. Mental assest kept in the mind of the offeree has no legal effect whatsoever.
3. **By the offeree Only:**
	- Only the person to whom the offer was made has the legal capacity to accpet.
	- A third part's acceptance is void.
4. **Within Reasonable Time:**
	- Acceptance must be given before the offer lappes.
	- Unreasonable delay destroys the offer and a belated acceptance creates not contact.
5. **Prescribed  Manner:**
	- If the offer specifies a particular mode of acceptance such is by registered post or email the offeree must follow that made.
	- Acceptance given in any other manner may not bind the offer.
	- If no mode is prescribed, acceptance must be communicated  in a reasonable and usual manner for that type of transaction.
6. **Silence is not Acceptance:**
	- Silence, inaction or failure to respond does not amount to acceptance in Indian Law.

## Consideration
- When at the desire of the promisor, the promise or any other person has done, abstained from doing does, abstains from doing or promise is called consideration for the promise.
- Consideration is something given in return for a promise. It is the price paid for the promise of another. Without consideration an agreement is generally not enforceable as a contract.

#### Essential Elements of Valid consideration
- **Must move at the desire of the promisor:**
	- The act should be done because of the promisor wanted it.
	- **Example:**
		- A asks B to paint his house and promises ₹10,00 (Valid consideration)
- **May move from promise or another person:**
	- Consideration can be given by the promise or even a third person.
	- **Example:**
		- A promises sell land to B, but payment is made by C (contract may be still valid).
- **May be Past, Present or Future:**
	- Past Consideration - Service already done before promise.
	- Present (Executed) - Immediate exchange.
	- Future (Executory) - Promise for future performance.
- **Need to be Adequate:**
	- the law does not require equal value.
	- **Example:**
		- Selling a watch worth ₹5,000 for ₹500 can still be valid if consent is free.

#### Exceptions where an agreement is valid even without consideration
1. **Natural love and affection:**
	- An agreement made out of natural love and affection between close relatives in writing and registered is valid even without consideration.
	- **Example:**
		- A father promises in a registered written document to give ₹2 lakh to his daughter  out of love and affection. The agreement is valid even through the daughter given nothing in return.
2. **Compensation for Past Voluntary Service:**
	- A promise to compensation a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor is valid.
	- **Example:**
		- A finds B's lost bag and return it. Later B promise to pay A ₹5,000. This promise is enforceable even though A acted before the promise.
3. **Promise to Pay a Time Bared Debt #missingword If:**
	- The debt cannot be recovered because the limitation period expired. and the debtor given a written and signal promise to pay.
	- The argument is valid without #missingword  consideration.
	- **Example:**
		- A owns B ₹20,000 but the legal time limit for recovery has expired. A later signs a written promise to repay. B can enforce it.

## Performance Of Contracts
- Performance is the normal mode of discharge of a contract. If the parties of a contract have done what they had obligated to do as per the terms of the contract, the contract is accomplished of legal obligations created by the contract.
- If all the parties of the contract fulfill their respective obligations, it is the usual and natural mode of discharge or terminating contract.

### Offer of Performance or Tender
- According to **section 37** of act, "The parties to a contract must either perform or offer to perform their respective promise, unless such performance is dispensed with or excused under the provision of this act, or of any other law."
- **Performance may be of Two types:**
	- **Actual performance**
		- Actual performance is the accomplishment of the obligation arising from a contract, by the parties in accordance with the terms of the contract.
		- **Example:**
			- X promises to sell a car to Y for 1 lakh. When X delivers the car to Y and Y pays 1 lakh to X as per the term of the contract, the contract is discharged by actual performanc.
	- **Offer to performance**, also known as **attempted/Tender performance:**
		- When a party to a contract offer to perform his obligations under the contract, but is not able to do as the other party to the contract prevents him form preforming  the obligation. 
		- It is called attempted performance or tender of performance.
		- If the offer of performance made by the promisor is not accepted by the promise, the obligation of the party making the tender is discharged and he is not liable for non performance, although his right still #missingword 
		- A valid tender of performance  is equal to actual performance.
		- However, in case of tender of money a valid tender does not discharge the debt.

### Essential of a Valid Tender
- A valid tender of performance must fulfill the following:
- **It must be unconditional:**
	- A valid tender of performance must be unconditional. A tender becomes  conditional if it is not as per the terms of the contract.
		- **Example:**
			- X, who owes money to Y, offers to pay the amount he owes on the condition that X should sell him his car for ₹50,000.
			- this is not a valid tender.
- **It must be made at a porper time and place:**
	- Tender of performance must be made at appropriate time and correct location.
	- In case the contract mentions the time and place of performance, the offer of performance must be made at the proper time and place mentioned.
	- If no time and place is mentioned in the contract by the parties, tender at the place of business and during business hours is considered proper.
		- **Example:**
			- X is a tenant of Y. He offers to sell a car at a cinema theater.
			- Y is not bound to accept this tender if it is not made at a proper place.
- **It must be in the proper form:**
	- A tender of money must be in currency notes or coins and not in foreign currency.
	- A person is not a required to accept a cheque. But a payment  by cheque is valid tender if the person to whom offer is made is ready and willing to accept it.
- **It must be of the whole obligation:**
	- To be a valid tender, it must be of the whole obligation contracted for, Partial fulfillment of promise
	- i.e. part of payment or part of delivery of the good contracted for is not a valid tender.
	- If no other arrangements is made a debtor who owes money must pay the entire amount including interest at once and the same time.
- **The person making the tender must be willing to perform:**
	- Tender of performance must be made by a person who is able and willing to perform the promise.
- **In case of tender of goods, reasonable opportunity to the promise for Inspection of goods:**
	- the promise must have a reasonable opportunity to verify that the thing offered is the thing which the promisor is bound by his promise to deliver.
	- **Example:**
		- A contract to deliver to B 100kg of first quality rice.
		- In order to execute an offer of performance. A must give reasonable opportunity of satisfying himself that the thing offered is rice of first quality contracted  for and that there are 100kg.
- **It must be made to the proper person:**
	- It must be made to the promise or his dully authorized agent.
- **Offer to one of the joint promiseor:**
	- An offer to one of the several joint promise has the same legal effect as an offer to all of them.
	- **Example:**
		- A payment made to a partner is deemed to be a payment to the partnership firms.
